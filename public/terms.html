1. Intended Use & Premise

NoCharity is an independent, open-source project designed to explore relative wealth mathematics. Inspired by a comedic premise from stand-up comedian Gianmarco Soresi, the application computes what publicly reported billionaire philanthropic contributions look like when scaled proportionally to an everyday household's income or net worth.

The site is intended strictly for personal, educational, journalistic, and satirical reflection. It provides context on the sheer scale of extreme wealth through mathematical proportionality.

2. Informational & Satirical Purpose

NoCharity operates at the intersection of public data math and comedic critique. The tool converts massive abstract sums (e.g., millions or billions of dollars) into intuitive personal equivalents (e.g., buying a cup of coffee or a lunch combo).

While the mathematical engine strives for precise calculation, the contextual framing is light-hearted and satirical. It is designed to foster critical thinking about wealth concentration and charitable pledges, not to serve as an authoritative financial balance sheet.

3. Limitations of Public-Source Data

NoCharity relies entirely on publicly available data sources, including:

  • Regulatory filings with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC Form 4, 13D/G).
  • IRS Form 990-PF foundation disclosures and registered charity filings.
  • Real-time wealth indices published by financial outlets, such as the Forbes Real-Time Billionaires List and Bloomberg Billionaires Index.
  • Public registries, such as The Giving Pledge and official philanthropic press releases.

Important Realities of Wealth Reporting: Public wealth estimates are inherently volatile, subject to fluctuating stock prices, private equity valuations, currency shifts, and differences in valuation methodologies between indices. Furthermore, charitable gift disclosures are frequently incomplete, revised retrospectively, pledged over multi-year periods, or disputed among multiple parties.

Discrepancy Policy: When credible public sources disagree on a donation amount or net worth figure, NoCharity flags the discrepancy transparently in Advanced Mode rather than inventing figures or picking favorites.
