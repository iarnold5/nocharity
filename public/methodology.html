⏳ 1. Historical Wealth & The Denominator Problem

The single most common flaw in philanthropic analysis is dividing historical donations by a billionaire's current net worth.

The Denominator Fallacy:
Suppose an investor donated $1,000,000,000 in 2006 when their net worth was $25,000,000,000. That represented 4.00% of their entire fortune at that moment. If that individual's fortune subsequently expands to $150,000,000,000 due to market appreciation by 2026, comparing the 2006 donation to their 2026 wealth produces 0.67%, artificially minimizing their relative sacrifice. Conversely, evaluating an older donation against a collapsed current fortune creates false inflation.

NoCharity enforces Historical Wealth-Aware Parity. Every qualifying donation is evaluated against the donor's documented net worth at or immediately preceding the donation date:

Proximity Tier Calendar Distance Quality Classification Methodological Handling
EXACT_DATE 0 Days (Same Date) Highest Benchmark Direct valuation snapshot recorded on the date of transaction.
NEAR_DATE 1 to 90 Days Prior Audited High Quality Snapshot within 1 quarter prior; market fluctuations are minimal.
SAME_YEAR 91 to 365 Days Prior Documented Estimate Snapshot recorded earlier in the same calendar year.
APPROXIMATE 366 to 1,095 Days Prior Approximate Estimate Multi-year baseline (≤ 3 years). Explicitly labeled approximate.
UNAVAILABLE > 3 Years or Future Rejected (No Parity) Calculations suppressed. Future snapshots are strictly disallowed.
Anti-Nonsense Rule: Temporal Consistency
If a donation took place in 2018, NoCharity will never silently use a 2026 wealth snapshot. Using a valuation date far into the future relative to the donation date without explicit disclaimer produces misleading results. Any such temporal gap is flagged, suppressed, or marked approximate.

No Fabricated Interpolation: Financial benchmarks (Bloomberg Billionaires Index, Forbes) provide discrete point-in-time valuation snapshots. Generating smooth polynomial or spline curves between distant snapshot years introduces false precision. NoCharity uses discrete documented snapshots only.

📊 2. Peak Donation vs. The Invalid Lifetime Giving Ratio

A frequent request in billionaire philanthropy trackers is a single "lifetime giving percentage." In early prototypes or unvetted tools, this is often calculated as:

The Invalid Lifetime Ratio Fallacy:
Total Cumulative Lifetime Giving ÷ Current (Latest) Net Worth

This calculation is conceptually and mathematically invalid. A donation made in 1998 had a real historical denominator: the donor's wealth in 1998. Adding gifts from 1998, 2010, and 2024 together in the numerator while dividing by a 2026 wealth snapshot produces a fictitious number. If the donor's fortune has multiplied tenfold, the ratio severely underestimates their historical generosity. If their fortune has shrunk, the ratio artificially inflates.

NoCharity has completely eliminated the invalid lifetime giving ratio from all public rankings, sorting, headline metrics, and API responses. We do not replace it with another misleading cumulative ratio.

Instead, when NoCharity presents a headline percentage metric, we display:

  • Peak Donation / Wealth (%): The highest percentage of wealth given in any single documented personal donation event, calculated against the verified wealth snapshot at that donation date.
  • Total Documented Personal Giving: The absolute nominal sum of all qualifying, confirmed personal gifts.
  • Largest Single Personal Donation: The largest individual gift in nominal terms.
  • Documented Qualifying Donation Count: The number of discrete personal transactions meeting audit criteria.

All figures are explicitly labeled as documented qualifying donations, not an exhaustive record of all lifetime charity.

🧮 3. User Income Comparison Methodology

NoCharity allows visitors to input their monthly take-home pay or annual household income to see a proportional comparison against billionaire gifts.

Methodological Declaration:
"Equivalent by percentage of wealth."
This comparison calculates the donation as a percentage of the billionaire's wealth at the time of the donation, then applies that exact percentage to the user's income.

What this comparison IS NOT:
  • It is not an estimate of the billionaire's annual salary or income.
  • It is not an estimate of disposable cash flow.
  • It does not infer where the billionaire obtained the donation funds.

Context-Aware Sacrifice & Currency Handling:

  • USD Tangible Comparisons: For USD inputs, the application references everyday items (e.g., a cup of coffee, a grocery run) to make microscopic percentages intuitive.
  • International Currencies (e.g. INR): NoCharity never blindly converts USD dollar thresholds into foreign currencies without appropriate local calibration. For non-USD currencies, the application delivers respectful proportional statements (e.g., "Proportional income equivalent: ₹450") rather than inappropriate named items.
  • Neutral, Non-Moralizing Phrasing: Context-sensitive descriptors adapt based on the relative burden ("That's your proportional equivalent", "That's a substantial personal sacrifice at your income level", "That would consume a large share of your income") without lecturing or providing financial advice.

🏛️ 4. Personal vs. Organization Giving

Public relations releases routinely conflate three entirely distinct categories of philanthropic capital:

Attribution Category Funding Source Tax & Economic Reality NoCharity Treatment
PERSONAL_DIRECT Individual Personal Accounts Direct outlay of private assets (cash, personal stock shares). Qualifies for Personal Parity. Tracked as true personal sacrifice.
FOUNDATION Private Charitable Foundation Assets already legally irrevocably gifted; donor took tax deduction years earlier. Excluded from Personal Parity. Displayed exclusively in Associated Organization section.
CORPORATE Company Corporate Treasury Company funds owned by all shareholders, not billionaire's personal checkbook. Excluded from Personal Parity. Displayed as corporate philanthropy context.
DAF Donor-Advised Fund Intermediary sponsor (Fidelity/Schwab); irrevocable tax write-off already captured. Excluded from Personal Parity. Disclosed as intermediary transfer.
PLEDGE Future Commitment Non-binding public pledge (e.g. Giving Pledge); no asset transfer completed. Excluded from All Giving Totals. Pledges do not count as completed giving.
The Foundation Double-Dipping Defense:
When a billionaire transfers $1,000,000,000 of stock to their private family foundation, they receive an immediate personal income tax deduction of up to 30% of their adjusted gross income. When that foundation subsequently writes a $10,000,000 check to a food bank five years later, that $10,000,000 comes from foundation endowment money—not the billionaire's personal checking account. Counting both the original contribution and every subsequent foundation grant as "personal billionaire giving" double-counts the same dollar.

✅ 5. Source Hierarchy & The Evidence Ladder

No transaction enters NoCharity's confirmed database without passing an evidentiary audit. We structure evidence into a clear three-tier hierarchy:

Hierarchy Tier Source Type Required Primary Evidence Verification Handling
Tier 1: PRIMARY Regulatory Filings SEC Form 4 (insider equity disposition), IRS Form 990-PF (foundation schedule of contributors), or audited grant contracts. Highest quality score (+20 pts). Automatically eligible for all calculations.
Tier 2: REPUTABLE Investigative Journalism Institutional recipient disclosures (e.g. university endowment announcements) or dual-sourced investigative reporting (AP, Reuters, ProPublica). High confidence (+12 pts). Eligible for personal parity if funding vehicle is confirmed.
Tier 3: DISCOVERY Automated Ingestion Google News RSS feeds, wire blurbs, or preliminary press releases. Marked unverified. Preliminary score (+5 pts). Ineligible for personal giving calculations until verified by maintainer.
Maintainer Verification Security:
Candidate discovery records cannot be mutated or verified publicly. The discovery review endpoint (POST /api/advanced/discover/verify) requires an authenticated maintainer token via environment variable (MAINTAINER_TOKEN). The authoritative method for vetting and committing discovered records is the offline CLI review tool (node scripts/review-discovery.js).

📜 6. Historical Figures & The Nominal Currency Standard

Historical figures like Andrew Carnegie and John D. Rockefeller present unique analytical challenges. In 1901, Andrew Carnegie sold Carnegie Steel to J.P. Morgan for $480 million, retaining approximately $350 million in personal net worth.

The Nominal Currency Invariant:
NoCharity records historical figures strictly in historical nominal USD.

We never silently multiply historical nominal values by arbitrary inflation factors or substitute modern trillion-dollar valuations into historical databases. Andrew Carnegie's baseline wealth is recorded as $350,000,000 nominal USD (1901), and his gifts (e.g., $10,000,000 to the Carnegie Institute in 1901, $125,000,000 to the Carnegie Corporation in 1911) are evaluated in historical nominal USD.

Temporal Parity for Historical Donors:

  • 1901 Gift ($10,000,000): Evaluated against Carnegie's March 1901 wealth snapshot ($350,000,000). The gap is 258 days within the same calendar year (SAME_YEAR), producing an authentic historical giving ratio of 2.86%.
  • 1911 Gift ($125,000,000): Carnegie's closest documented prior wealth snapshot is 1901 (10 years prior). Because NoCharity enforces a strict 3-year credibility ceiling and never searches into the future, this 1911 gift is classified as UNAVAILABLE for percentage parity. It remains fully documented in absolute dollars.

📑 7. Source Limitations & Filing Lags

Transparency requires acknowledging the structural constraints of open public data:

  • IRS Form 990-PF Reporting Lags: Non-profit private foundations file annual tax returns up to 10.5 months after their fiscal year ends. With IRS processing delays, official government filings frequently lag actual grant disbursals by 12 to 24 months.
  • SEC Form 4 Scope Limits: SEC Form 4 filings capture transfers of public company common equity by corporate insiders. They do not capture gifts of cash, privately held business equity, municipal bonds, artwork, or real estate holdings.
  • Donor-Advised Fund Redactions: Under federal law, commercial DAF sponsors (e.g. Fidelity Charitable, Schwab Charitable) are legally designated as 501(c)(3) public charities. Grants made from a donor's DAF account are filed on the sponsor's master Form 990 without identifying the underlying donor.

🔍 8. Incomplete Public Records & Floor Metrics

The public record is inherently incomplete. Billionaires who value privacy, support contentious causes, or donate to religious institutions, local relief efforts, or medical funds without issuing press releases leave minimal regulatory paper trails.

The PR Bias:
A public database naturally indexes what is public. A media-savvy billionaire who employs a public relations team to announce every gift will generate a more extensive documented trail than an introverted peer of equal or greater generosity. NoCharity documents disclosures; it does not measure moral virtue.

⚖️ 9. Why "Documented Giving" is NOT Equivalent to Total Real-World Giving

Every figure in NoCharity Advanced Mode represents a documented minimum (floor), not a definitive ceiling.

The "Documented Only" Invariant:
If NoCharity reports that a billionaire has $15,000,000,000 in documented personal giving, this statement means exactly:
"Public regulatory filings and audited disclosures confirm at least $15.0B in qualifying personal transactions."

It does not mean the billionaire has given only $15.0B. Nor does a blank record prove that an unranked billionaire has given zero dollars. A lower documented total does not prove lesser real-world charity.

When NoCharity calculates parity ratios, it explicitly qualifies:
"Proportional wealth comparison based on documented wealth at donation date. This is an illustration of relative proportional scale, not an assertion of identical subjective financial sacrifice."

🎯 10. The 8 Essential Questions & Data Quality Heuristic Formula

To keep data evaluation honest and transparent, NoCharity calculates an automated Data Quality Score (0 to 100) for every donation record.

Definition:
"A NoCharity data-quality heuristic based on source, verification, attribution, and historical-data confidence."

The Data Quality Score is not an audited financial rating, not an official regulatory score, and not absolute proof of underlying accounting validity. It is a programmatic heuristic measuring the completeness, verification depth, and temporal precision of the record.
1. WHO? Donor identity: named person or verified organization entity.
2. WHAT? Nature of the gift (monetary, equity, real estate) and stated cause.
3. WHEN? Valid calendar date in parseable YYYY-MM-DD ISO format.
4. HOW MUCH? Finite, positive dollar amount with explicit ISO currency.
5. FROM WHOM? Funding vehicle: personal, foundation, company, DAF, or pledge.
6. TO WHOM? Recipient charity, institutional fund, university, or hospital.
7. SOURCE? Verifiable regulatory filing link or audited publisher citation.
8. HOW CERTAIN? Evidentiary status tier: VERIFIED, PARTIALLY_VERIFIED, UNVERIFIED.

Detailed Scoring Breakdown (100 Point Scale):

Evaluation Pillar Maximum Points Scoring Criteria & Deductions
1. Completeness of 8 Questions 40 Points 5 points awarded per answered question (WHO, WHAT, WHEN, HOW MUCH, FROM WHOM, TO WHOM, SOURCE, HOW CERTAIN).
2. Verification Status Quality 30 Points VERIFIED: 30 pts | PARTIALLY_VERIFIED: 20 pts | UNVERIFIED: 10 pts | DISCOVERED: 5 pts | DISPUTED: 0 pts
3. Source Provenance 20 Points Tier 1 Regulatory Filing / Verified Primary: 20 pts | Tier 2 Secondary Reporting / URL: 12 pts | Named Publisher Only: 6 pts | Missing Citation: 0 pts
4. Temporal Wealth Consistency 10 Points Snapshot ≤ 90 days or same calendar year (EXACT / NEAR / SAME_YEAR): +10 pts | Snapshot 1–3 years prior (APPROXIMATE): +5 pts | Temporal gap > 3 years or future snapshot (UNAVAILABLE): -10 pts
5. Invariant Deductions -35 Points Discrepant source amounts detected: -15 pts | Core schema validation failure: -20 pts

Automated Quality Grades:

Quality Grade Score Threshold Status Label Audit Standards Required
Grade A 85 – 100 Audited & Complete Answers all 8 questions, verified primary regulatory source, zero discrepancies.
Grade B 70 – 84 Well-Documented High completeness, confirmed recipient disclosure or reliable dual reporting.
Grade C 50 – 69 Preliminary / Unverified Preliminary public reporting or approximate temporal wealth benchmark.
Grade D / Flagged < 50 or Flagged Disputed / Incomplete Active source amount disagreements, missing donor entity, or contested claim.
Multi-Source Discrepancy Rule:
If two credible sources disagree on a donation amount (for example, a press release claiming $1.2B while an SEC Form 4 indicates $1.0B in shares transferred), NoCharity never silently picks one. The disagreement is preserved on the record, both figures are recorded, and the donation is flagged with a discrepancy notice.
