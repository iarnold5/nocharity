1. Historical Wealth Matching

Gifts are evaluated strictly against documented wealth at the time of donation. A 2018 gift cannot silently use a 2026 valuation snapshot without explicitly marking it as approximate or unavailable.

2. Personal vs Organization Isolation

Foundation disbursements, corporate treasury giving, and DAF transfers are tracked for context but quarantined completely from individual sacrifice and parity calculations.

3. Multi-Source Discrepancies

If credible sources disagree on gift amounts, NoCharity does not silently pick one. Both figures and the spread are permanently retained and flagged with Grade D.

4. Pledge & Dispute Quarantine

Non-binding pledges (Giving Pledge) do not count as completed giving until legally fulfilled. Disputed gifts are strictly excluded from default rankings.

5. Public Records Limitations

Public disclosures only cover regulated gifts (SEC Form 4 stock transfers, IRS 990-PF grants). Private cash, anonymous DAFs, and non-US jurisdictions have inherent data blind spots.

6. Documented vs Real-World Giving

"Documented giving" is not total giving. A billionaire with $2B documented gave at least $2B, whereas an undocumented billionaire cannot be claimed to have given nothing. Coverage indicators clarify time horizons.

🛡️ Research Standards

Data & Methodology

How NoCharity measures relative sacrifice and maintains public disclosure data

Primary Filing Anchor

NoCharity benchmarks billionaire philanthropy against regulatory filings (SEC Form 4 beneficial ownership changes, IRS Form 990-PF private foundation tax returns) anchored to point-in-time net worth on the exact date of gift.

Independent Auditing

Each transaction record is verified across 8 core qualitative criteria (donor, recipient, date, exact dollar amount, funding source, regulatory filing link, valuation snapshot proximity, and verification status).

Strict Attribution Isolation

A central failure of mainstream philanthropy reporting is conflating individual sacrifice with corporate balance sheet donations or foundation grants:

  • Personal Direct (Eligible): Direct personal checks or stock grants transferred out of personal assets.
  • Foundation Disbursements (Excluded from personal): Grants made by 501(c)(3) tax-exempt private endowments funded in prior tax years.
  • Corporate Treasury (Excluded): Philanthropic sponsorships paid from public company revenues belonging to all shareholders.
  • Donor-Advised Funds (DAF) (Isolated): Intermediary tax shelters that obscure timing and recipient payouts.
  • Giving Pledges (Quarantined): Non-binding public letters that carry zero legal enforcement.

Why Wealth-to-Wealth First?

Billionaires do not fund charitable initiatives from a bi-weekly paycheck; their gifts originate from accumulated capital assets. Comparing gifts against wealth first and then scaling that proportion to everyday assets prevents misleading income comparisons.

Temporal Proximity Tiers

Point-in-time valuation snapshots are classified by confidence: Exact Date, High Proximity (±30 days), Annual Benchmark (±180 days), Stale Proxy (1–3 years), and Quarantined (>3 years or future-dated).

Source Hierarchy

Tier 1: Regulatory Filings (SEC EDGAR, IRS 990-PF, Charity Commission UK, MCA India).

Tier 2: Primary Audited Disclosures & Verified Press Releases with transaction details.

Tier 3: Investigative News Reports (Reuters, Bloomberg, Associated Press, Forbes).

Documented vs Undocumented Giving

Public regulatory records cannot capture private anonymous cash donations. A billionaire with $500M documented gave at least $500M, while an individual without filings cannot be definitively stated to have given zero. We report only what can be proven.

Satirical Intent

NoCharity juxtaposes headline-grabbing figures against the scale of astronomical fortunes. The mathematical comparison is rigorous; the editorial perspective is unapologetically satirical.
